    01

    Nepal · 2014 – 2023

    Mapping from the ground up

    From a geomatics degree at Tribhuvan University to leading NAXA's GIS and remote sensing team across Nepal, Kenya and Eswatini.

    I trained as a geomatics engineer at the Institute of Engineering, Tribhuvan University (BE, 2019), and joined NAXA in Kathmandu in 2018. Over five years I went from flying drones to leading the company's GIS and remote sensing work.

    The work was always in service of a decision someone had to make on the ground. I mapped open spaces that emergency responders could use after an earthquake, for IOM. I modelled where six cereal crops could grow in Nepal's hills under the climate of 2060 and 2100. In Kenya I ran participatory mapping of how far rural people travel to markets and clinics, and in Eswatini I helped monitor drought from thirty years of satellite data. I also built the GIS behind Karnali Province's climate-change information system and its agro-meteorological centres, a disaster and climate resilience plan for Rapti Sonari, glacial flood-risk studies and solar-site suitability models.

    More than 100 UAV flights and 50 communities taught me that a map is only useful if the people in it can act on it.

        Florida Atlantic University · 2023 – 2024

        A new coast

        Arriving in Boca Raton in August 2023, and finding that the questions from Nepal travel well.

        I came to Florida Atlantic University on a full academic scholarship for the MS–PhD track in Civil, Environmental and Geomatics Engineering, and joined the Center for Water Resiliency and Risk Reduction (CWR³) as a graduate research assistant.

        Three months in, I won first place at FAU's GIS Day presentation competition with a participatory-GIS mobility plan for Birendranagar, Surkhet. The next summer I received the USGIF Maxar Scholarship for Diversity and Innovation in GEOINT, which USGIF gives to one student in the country, for work on fusing drone imagery and LiDAR to reconstruct cities in 3D.

        I finished the MS in Civil Engineering (Geospatial Systems) in December 2024 and began the PhD in January 2025.

            03

            CWR³ · 2023 – present

            Teaching computers to see water

            Making watershed master plans repeatable, from ArcPy notebooks to a GPU flood engine nine times faster.

            Florida cities plan for flooding through watershed master plans: dozens of basins, rainfall, sea-level-rise and tide scenarios, and LiDAR terrain that has to be processed the same way every time. I lead the geospatial side of these plans at CWR³ for more than 12 communities.

            HialeahHomesteadNorth Miami Miami-Dade CountyBroward CountyPolk CountySanta Rosa County

            The first step was automating the existing workflow in ArcGIS Pro. That work became my lead-author paper in Natural Hazards (2025). The second was PyWMP, a Python watershed modelling platform whose design I lead. It brings HEC-HMS methods and Cascade 2001 routing into one framework, with 1D, 2D and hybrid models that run on a CPU or a GPU. It matches the South Florida Water Management District's Cascade 2001 worked examples to within 0.5%. On a 275,617-cell model of Blackwater Creek, the GPU cut a 100-year storm run from 100.9 to 11.1 minutes.

            Alongside the engineering work I study the science: graph neural networks that learn how rainfall becomes runoff across connected watersheds, and a 43-year record of how Florida's vegetation responds to its climate.

                04

                ASPRS FAU Student Chapter · August 2024 – present

                Building a room for geospatial people

                The chapter formed in August 2024. A year later it was funded, had more than 200 followers, and was sending free flood data to Nepal.

                I have been president of the FAU student chapter of the American Society for Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing since it was chartered in August 2024, and was re-elected for 2026–27. The first year was spent setting up: Geo Week in Denver, the FL-ASPRS booth at the South Florida GIS Expo, and putting laser scanners, drones and multispectral sensors in front of first-year students at the Owl Involved fair.

                In the second year the chapter ran its first large event. World GIS Day, on November 17, 2025, was co-hosted with the FWEA and FSMS student chapters and CWR³. It brought in about 100 people, with faculty and industry talks from FDOT, CDM Smith, Caulfield & Wheeler and Digital E Consulting. A student research competition paid out $850 in prizes, and Esri donated three software licenses.

                Back to Nepal

                On August 26, 2026, a dam breach on the Lhende Khola sent a flash flood down Nepal's Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor. It destroyed bridges, roads, a border post and six hydropower plants. Within days our chapter compared before-and-after Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope imagery along about 60 km of the river and mapped roughly 82 hectares of changed ground. We ranked 11 candidate search zones and released the layers free to the teams working there.

                The method came from the SAR and optical flood-mapping research behind FloodFusion and our 2026 Himalayan flood-detection paper. Nepal to Florida and back to Nepal.

                    05

                    CWR³Data · 2025 – 2026

                    One door to the data

                    Everything the lab learned about watershed data, in one platform, with a public beta at GIS Mela.

                    Each watershed plan began with the same search for elevation, rainfall, soils and gauge records in a dozen places, and ended with results that were hard to share. CWR³Data is my answer: the digital platform of FAU's Center for Water Resiliency and Risk Reduction. It puts a geospatial data portal and a hydrological modelling workbench in one browser tab.

                    • Data CatalogSearch, preview and request Florida water data, with standard metadata.
                    • PyWMP-WEB1D, 2D and hybrid flood models in the browser, running on the GPU.
                    • AeroFuseDrone imagery and 3DEP LiDAR turned into terrain models, orthophotos and change maps.
                    • NotebooksGeoAI on the GPU, next to the data.

                    Public beta · launching

                    CWR³Data goes public at GIS Mela, November 17, 2026

                    GIS Mela is World GIS Day at FAU: a one-day festival of maps, data and the people who make them, held 9 AM–3 PM at the Student Union. The CWR³Data public beta launches there.

                        2026 and onward

                        Open threads

                        Three research questions I'm still working on, and an invitation to work on them together.

                        Can a model learn the river network? ESTGCN encodes the upstream–downstream topology of HUC12 watersheds into a graph neural network, and asks whether it beats a graph built only from distance. So far it does. Code · manuscript under review.

                        How is Florida's green changing? A 43-year analysis of NDVI against temperature and precipitation, separating long-term climate signals from hurricanes and drought. Code · manuscript under review.

                        Can one open framework do 1D, 2D and hybrid flood modelling? The PyWMP framework paper. Manuscript under review.

                        Outside hydrology, I'm applying the same spatial methods to Nepal's digital election readiness (SSRN preprint, 2026). If you're hiring, recruiting a postdoc, or want to build something with open water data, get in touch.
